    The driving of vibrational motion by external electric fields is a topic of continued interest, due to the possibility of assessing new or metastable material phases with desirable properties. Here, we combine ab initio molecular dynamics within the electric-dipole approximation with machine-learning neural networks (NNs) to develop a general, efficient and accurate method to perform electric-field-driven nuclear dynamics for molecules, solids, and liquids.     We report a comprehensive muon spin rotation and relaxation (muSR) study of the hexagonal 6H-perovskites Ba3LnRu2O9 (Ln = Gd, Ho) to investigate the role of rare-earth anisotropy in the magnetic ground state of 4d-4f coupled systems. These results establish that the rare-earth ion in Ba3LnRu2O9 is a powerful tuning parameter, driving the ground state from homogeneous to complex, multi-scale inhomogeneous behavior through competing 4d-4f magnetic interactions.

    Dopants can tune the activity of zirconia-based CO₂ hydrogenation catalysts through changes in adsorbate and transition-state (TS) energetics. Computing these quantities with density functional theory (DFT) becomes expensive when several dopants, reaction intermediates, and TSs must be screened. Here, we further develop the graph-based machine learning method Wasserstein Weisfeiler-Lehman Gaussian Process Regression (WWL-GPR): we introduce additional electronic-structure and local-environment features relevant for oxide surfaces and use the resulting model to predict adsorption and TS energies on ZrO₂(101) surfaces doped with Al³⁺, Cd²⁺, Ga³⁺, In³⁺, and Zn²⁺. Adsorbate-surface structures are represented as attributed graphs that combine bonds connectivity and features. We benchmark WWL-GPR against thermodynamic scaling relations (TSR) for reaction intermediates and Bronsted-Evans-Polanyi relations (BEP) for TSs, and then propagate the predicted energies to microkinetic CH₃OH-yield estimates. WWL-GPR consistently improves the prediction of reaction intermediate energetics relative to linear scaling approaches and provides TS estimates that are competitive with BEP-based models. These improvements become especially relevant when the predicted energetics are propagated through the reaction network. Microkinetic calculations show that WWL-GPR-derived energies give CH₃OH yields closer to DFT-derived yields than the linear-relation workflow. Degree-of-rate-control analysis links the activity errors to dopant-dependent rate-controlling steps, including HCOO* formation, H₂COOH* dissociation, and H₃CO* formation. These results support WWL-GPR as a data-efficient surrogate for connecting atomistic energetics with catalyst-screening observables on doped oxide surfaces.

We recover every rotation-equivariant convolution considered previously through constraints on the kernel, spin choices and inclusion or omission of the lift and projection.
