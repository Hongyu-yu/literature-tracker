    异构系统的机器学习哈密顿量极化#
    Machine Learning Hamiltonians with Polarizations for Heterogeneous Systems#
    作者：Jia-Yu Zhou、Wentao Zhang、Mouyi Weng、Zhao-Yi Yan、Lin-Wang Wang。arXiv · physics，2026-10-28
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配6

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：机器学习哈密顿量使用局部原子结构来确定局部哈密顿矩阵元素，而无需进行自洽计算。它们已被用于研究大型系统并对其电子和光学特性进行高通量筛选。哈密顿量的预测本质上是电子静电势能的预测；然而，对于异质系统，这种势能通常由远处的特征决定，例如界面或表面偶极子，甚至纳米器件中的施加电压。因此，它不能仅由局部原子结构决定。从这个意义上说，需要使用本征原子偶极子、施加的外部势能和极化率自洽地求解电子静电势能。幸运的是，固有偶极子和极化率是局部属性，而施加的势能是规定的输入。在这项工作中，我们提出了 DeepH-Polar，这是一种物理信息机器学习哈密顿模型，它明确预测本征原子偶极子和极化张量，并使用它们自洽地求解电子静电势能。极性 GaN 系统和晶体-Si/非晶-SiO2 界面的基准测试表明，哈密顿量精度得到了提高，厚度相关静电变化的重现性得到了改善，并且没有极化的模型无法捕获空间不均匀场感应屏蔽。我们的模型还允许我们研究纳米级电子设备。通过迭代求解哈密顿本征问题并自洽更新自由载流子占据和静电势，我们模拟了 8,194 个原子的门控半导体器件。

    📖 英文原文

    Machine learning Hamiltonians use local atomic structures to determine local Hamiltonian matrix elements without carrying out self-consistent calculations. They have been used to study large systems and to carry out high-throughput screening for their electronic and optical properties. The prediction of the Hamiltonian is essentially the prediction of the electron electrostatic potential energy; however, for heterogeneous systems, this potential energy is often determined by faraway features, such as interface or surface dipoles, or even an applied voltage in a nanodevice. Thus, it cannot be determined by local atomic structures alone. In this sense, one needs to solve the electron electrostatic potential energy self-consistently using intrinsic atomic dipoles, the applied external potential energy, and polarizabilities. Fortunately, the intrinsic dipoles and polarizabilities are local properties, whereas the applied potential energy is a prescribed input. In this work, we propose DeepH-Polar, a physically informed machine learning Hamiltonian model that explicitly predicts intrinsic atomic dipoles and polarizability tensors and uses them to solve the electron electrostatic potential energy self-consistently. Benchmarks on polar GaN systems and crystalline-Si/amorphous-SiO₂ interfaces demonstrate improved Hamiltonian accuracy and reproduction of thickness-dependent electrostatic variations, as well as spatially nonuniform field-induced screening that models without polarization fail to capture. Our model also allows us to study nanoscale electronic devices. By iteratively solving the Hamiltonian eigenproblem and self-consistently updating free-carrier occupations and the electrostatic potential, we simulate an 8,194-atom gated semiconductor device.

    📖 英文原文

    Density functional theory (DFT) gives the electronic structure of materials, but its cost limits large-supercell calculations and materials screening. Machine-learned Hamiltonians bypass the self-consistent cycle but are usually system-specific; universal ones could cover new compositions and disordered solids. Such models must generalize across chemistry and keep downstream properties physical, while staying inexpensive for large cells. Here we introduce UniTB, a universal E(3) equivariant Hamiltonian representation framework that integrates physical priors rooted in density-functional tight-binding theory with deep learning. UniTB's network, which corrects a prior Hamiltonian, generalizes across chemistry through a mixture of experts routed by that prior and a charge-equilibration head as in self-consistent-charge tight binding. A projection bounds band shifts from the prior, suppressing spurious states and keeping downstream properties physical. We provide the fastest SO(2) tensor-product GPU kernels for the network. The variant UniTB-Flash assembles its prior from two-center tables at O(N) cost for N atoms, against O(Nlog N) on a grid. Trained on 29,242 crystals, UniTB reaches a 0.48 meV matrix-element error on nonmagnetic test crystals spanning 68 elements. Without amorphous training data, both models agree with DFT optics and conductivity of amorphous Ge-Sb-Te. UniTB-Flash ranks candidates with no significant difference from UniTB, whose property values are more accurate; together they screen 16,195 crystals for five application domains of industrial relevance.

The attention analysis showed higher weights for probe representations near the H atoms in Fe-based structures. These findings highlight the potential of integrating charge-density information for energy prediction and its task-dependent benefits.
