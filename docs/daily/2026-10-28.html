    📖 英文原文

    Density functional theory (DFT) gives the electronic structure of materials, but its cost limits large-supercell calculations and materials screening. Machine-learned Hamiltonians bypass the self-consistent cycle but are usually system-specific; universal ones could cover new compositions and disordered solids. Such models must generalize across chemistry and keep downstream properties physical, while staying inexpensive for large cells. Here we introduce UniTB, a universal E(3) equivariant Hamiltonian representation framework that integrates physical priors rooted in density-functional tight-binding theory with deep learning. UniTB's network, which corrects a prior Hamiltonian, generalizes across chemistry through a mixture of experts routed by that prior and a charge-equilibration head as in self-consistent-charge tight binding. A projection bounds band shifts from the prior, suppressing spurious states and keeping downstream properties physical. We provide the fastest SO(2) tensor-product GPU kernels for the network. Trained on 29,242 crystals, UniTB reaches a 0.48 meV matrix-element error on nonmagnetic test crystals spanning 68 elements. Without amorphous training data, both models agree with DFT optics and conductivity of amorphous Ge-Sb-Te. UniTB-Flash ranks candidates with no significant difference from UniTB, whose property values are more accurate; together they screen 16,195 crystals for five application domains of industrial relevance.

    Attention as Lattice Gauge Theory: Relations Interact Only Through Wilson Loops#
    作者：Aakash Sarkar、Ananya Mukherjee、Motoshige Sato。arXiv · physics，2026-10-28

    📖 英文原文

    Relations compose through the entity they share - Alice is Bob's mother and Bob is Carol's father, so Alice is Carol's grandmother - and the order matters, since a father's mother is not a mother's father; verifying a composed relation means closing the loop. We show that if each entity's coordinate frame may be rotated independently (per-entity frame freedom), three things follow. In the pure-gauge sector, relations that share no cycle cannot interact at all. The only gauge-invariant score of a triangle is a class function of its holonomy H = Tᵢⱼ Tⱼₖ Tₖᵢ - a Wilson loop. Here, we propose a physics-informed ensemble (PIE) learning framework that integrates fundamental structure-property relationships with processing routes and sustainability criteria for mechanism-based alloy design. This work introduces a general, physics-informed, and quantifiable framework for designing sustainable alloys under real-world constraints.

    文献研究：Machine Learning Hamiltonians with Polarizations for Heterogeneous Systems#
    Machine Learning Hamiltonians with Polarizations for Heterogeneous Systems#
    作者：Jia-Yu Zhou、Wentao Zhang、Mouyi Weng、Zhao-Yi Yan、Lin-Wang Wang。arXiv · physics，2026-10-28
    🤖 AI×科学 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配6
    📖 英文原文

    Machine learning Hamiltonians use local atomic structures to determine local Hamiltonian matrix elements without carrying out self-consistent calculations. They have been used to study large systems and to carry out high-throughput screening for their electronic and optical properties. The prediction of the Hamiltonian is essentially the prediction of the electron electrostatic potential energy; however, for heterogeneous systems, this potential energy is often determined by faraway features, such as interface or surface dipoles, or even an applied voltage in a nanodevice. Thus, it cannot be determined by local atomic structures alone. In this sense, one needs to solve the electron electrostatic potential energy self-consistently using intrinsic atomic dipoles, the applied external potential energy, and polarizabilities. Fortunately, the intrinsic dipoles and polarizabilities are local properties, whereas the applied potential energy is a prescribed input. In this work, we propose DeepH-Polar, a physically informed machine learning Hamiltonian model that explicitly predicts intrinsic atomic dipoles and polarizability tensors and uses them to solve the electron electrostatic potential energy self-consistently. Benchmarks on polar GaN systems and crystalline-Si/amorphous-SiO₂ interfaces demonstrate improved Hamiltonian accuracy and reproduction of thickness-dependent electrostatic variations, as well as spatially nonuniform field-induced screening that models without polarization fail to capture. Our model also allows us to study nanoscale electronic devices. By iteratively solving the Hamiltonian eigenproblem and self-consistently updating free-carrier occupations and the electrostatic potential, we simulate an 8,194-atom gated semiconductor device.
