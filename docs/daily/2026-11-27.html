    In a hybrid cavity, the controls that generate atom--cavity entanglement can differ from those that make the atom sensitive to a magnetic field. We study this distinction for a moving two-level atom in a Kerr optomechanical cavity with photon loss, atomic dephasing and shot-to-shot velocity variation. We propagate the density operator and its Zeeman derivative together, average both over the unrecorded velocity, and compute atom--cavity negativity and atom-only quantum Fisher information (QFI). At the fixed interrogation time and midpoint Hamiltonian controls, increased photon loss lowers negativity but raises atomic QFI across the sampled loss--dephasing grid. The entanglement response to Kerr coupling changes sign with the mean transit frequency. A Gaussian-process model trained on 56 trajectories yields test-set R² values of 0.915 for negativity and 0.934 for QFI.     Plasma electrolytic polishing (PeP) is an anodic finishing process in which surface asperities are dissolved beneath a vapor–gaseous envelope (VGE). Previous studies have mainly addressed voltage and electrolyte properties, whereas immersion conditions and stirring remain less explored. This study investigated the effects of immersion depth, electrolyte reusability, immersion cycle, and stirring on the PeP of TC4 alloy using a 27-run full factorial design. Surface roughness, gloss, morphology, and elemental composition were evaluated. Twenty-three regression models and a target-specific hybrid approach were compared for predicting Ra and gloss using stirring-stratified nine-fold cross-validation. SHAP analysis identified stirring as the most influential predictor for both responses, followed by depth for Ra and cycle for gloss.
