    利用深度学习从二维红外光谱推断跃迁偶极子#
    Inferring Transition-Dipoles from Two-Dimensional Infrared Spectroscopy with Deep Learning#
    作者：Hao-Yue Zhang、Rong-Hang Chen、Qing Ai。arXiv · physics，2026-10-15
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配5

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：二维红外 (2D IR) 光谱是一种强大的非线性技术，用于探测振动耦合、非谐性、相干性和分子动力学。在二维红外光谱编码的微观参数中，过渡偶极子是表征分子结构和控制光与物质相互作用的基本量。然而，从光谱数据重建这些量仍然是一个具有挑战性的逆问题。在这里，我们介绍了一种基于物理的卷积神经网络 (CNN) Spec2DipoleNet，它通过非厄米哈密顿量 (NHH) 方法生成的 2D 红外光谱进行训练。这种多任务 CNN 在物理驱动的掩蔽约束下重建过渡偶极子矩阵。在验证集上，该模型的均方根误差 (RMSE) 为 0.085，R² 为 0.97，归一化互相关为 0.98。它可靠地恢复与允许的跃迁相关的矩阵元素，而对于名义上禁止的跃迁则观察到较大的偏差。我们的结果表明，可以从二维红外光谱中定量推断跃迁偶极子算子，凸显了基于物理的深度学习从多维光谱数据中提取微观量子参数的潜力。

    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：molecular dynamics。

    📖 英文原文

    Two-dimensional infrared (2D IR) spectroscopy is a powerful nonlinear technique for probing vibrational couplings, anharmonicities, coherence, and molecular dynamics. Among the microscopic parameters encoded in 2D IR spectra, transition dipoles are fundamental quantities that characterize molecular structure and govern light-matter interactions. However, reconstructing these quantities from spectroscopic data remains a challenging inverse problem. Here, we introduce a physics-informed convolutional neural network (CNN), Spec2DipoleNet, trained on 2D IR spectra generated via the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian (NHH) method. This multi-task CNN reconstructs the transition dipole matrices under physically-motivated masking constraints. On the validation set, the model achieves a root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 0.085, an R² of 0.97, and a normalized cross-correlation of 0.98. It reliably recovers matrix elements associated with allowed transitions, while larger deviations are observed for nominally forbidden transitions. Our results suggest that transition dipole operators can be quantitatively inferred from 2D IR spectra, highlighting the potential of physics-informed deep learning for extracting microscopic quantum parameters from multidimensional spectroscopic data.

    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：interatomic potential、density functional theory。

    📖 英文原文

    Automated materials discovery requires evaluating large pools of candidate configurations, with density functional theory (DFT) as a major throughput bottleneck. Pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approximate DFT cheaply, but their errors vary by orders of magnitude across configurations, making it difficult to decide when to trust the MLIP predictions and when to fall back to DFT. We frame this decision as a risk-controlled selection problem and solve it within a conformal risk control (CRC) framework. Each configuration is mapped from atomic numbers and Cartesian coordinates to a fixed geometric descriptor, which a lightweight predictor uses to estimate an MLIP-DFT disagreement score in energy, average force, and maximum force. CRC then calibrates this score into a selection threshold for DFT labeling such that the MLIP error on the unselected configurations, which keep their MLIP labels, stays below a user-chosen fraction α of the expected error. Under an identical guarantee across eight dataset-MLIP pairs, our predictor routes 7% and 14% fewer configurations to DFT at α=0.3 than committee baselines. Because committee disagreement overlooks errors the MLIPs share, our gains are largest when the DFT labels and MLIP training data differ in DFT functional and basis set, and narrow when the two agree. Without model access, the structure-only predictor also ranks errors as well as the target MLIP's latent representation and outperforms widely used descriptors such as Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) and Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSF).

    📖 英文原文

    The Hohenberg-Kohn theorem establishes that, in principle, the ground state (GS) charge density contains all GS information of a many-electron system, such that all GS observables can be expressed as functionals of the GS charge density. Conventional Kohn-Sham density functional theory requires iterative solution of the self-consistent-field equations at substantial computational cost, motivating the development of deep learning surrogates for electronic structure calculations and, in turn, accelerating computer-aided materials design. Here, we propose AIDEN, an Atomic-Interaction Density Equivariant Network for solving real-space charge density. AIDEN separates the element-dependent one-center density from environment-induced density redistribution and represents the latter through complementary atom- and edge-centered tensor correlations. A continuous low-rank Gaussian decoder then reconstructs the density at arbitrary spatial coordinates while reusing atomic encodings independently of the evaluation grid. AIDEN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on periodic crystal benchmarks while remaining competitive for molecular systems, and further demonstrates zero-shot transferability across several structurally distinct out-of-distribution case studies. Furthermore, AIDEN provides substantially faster inference than both baseline models and full SCF calculations, enabling efficient charge density reconstruction for large-scale electronic structure calculations.

    The exact microscopic origin, symmetry, and thermal melting mechanism of the charge density wave (CDW) phase in TiSe₂ remain intensely debated, particularly regarding chiral structural order and multi-step phase transitions. Using large-scale molecular dynamics driven by an accurate machine-learning interatomic potential, we resolve the finite-temperature structural dynamics of the monolayer TiSe₂.     We introduce a compact Topological-Geometrical Descriptor (TGD) for planar two-dimensional carbon networks. Atomic coordinates are mapped to a periodic planar graph and represented through ring populations, topological-charge statistics, projected ring geometry, and dual-graph defect adjacency. The resulting modeling vector contains 19 physically interpretable features. We evaluate TGD on 120 published planar carbon allotropes using the DFT relative energy per atom with respect to graphene as the target. Repeated five-fold cross-validation with gradient boosting yields a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.0826 +/- 0.0138 eV/atom and R2 = 0.803 +/- 0.074. Under the same validation protocol, TGD gives a mean MAE of 0.0828 eV/atom, compared with 0.0929 eV/atom for a 203-feature Matminer representation. We further analyze 360 vacancy-defected graphene configurations spanning 12 vacancy concentrations from 2 to 24 percent. When entire vacancy concentrations are held out, TGD gives a pooled MAE of 0.269 eV/vacancy and R2 = 0.861, outperforming simple baselines based on vacancy concentration alone or non-hexagonal-ring fraction alone. Sensitivity tests show that the inferred edge sets are stable to moderate changes in the adaptive bond factor. Finally, the mean absolute topological charge is examined as a one-variable empirical surrogate for the allotrope dataset and is treated explicitly as dataset-specific rather than universal. These results show that TGD combines compact prediction with direct structural interpretation while making clear the present restriction to single-layer planar carbon networks.
