    Neural-Network Solutions to Real-Space Charge Density and Generalization#
    作者：Yuxuan Zeng、Tong Xie、Taoyuze Lv、Zhicheng Zhong。arXiv · cond-mat，2026-10-15
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉6方向匹配6
    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：electronic structure calculation、density functional theory。

    📖 英文原文

    The Hohenberg-Kohn theorem establishes that, in principle, the ground state (GS) charge density contains all GS information of a many-electron system, such that all GS observables can be expressed as functionals of the GS charge density. Conventional Kohn-Sham density functional theory requires iterative solution of the self-consistent-field equations at substantial computational cost, motivating the development of deep learning surrogates for electronic structure calculations and, in turn, accelerating computer-aided materials design. Here, we propose AIDEN, an Atomic-Interaction Density Equivariant Network for solving real-space charge density. AIDEN separates the element-dependent one-center density from environment-induced density redistribution and represents the latter through complementary atom- and edge-centered tensor correlations. A continuous low-rank Gaussian decoder then reconstructs the density at arbitrary spatial coordinates while reusing atomic encodings independently of the evaluation grid. AIDEN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on periodic crystal benchmarks while remaining competitive for molecular systems, and further demonstrates zero-shot transferability across several structurally distinct out-of-distribution case studies. Furthermore, AIDEN provides substantially faster inference than both baseline models and full SCF calculations, enabling efficient charge density reconstruction for large-scale electronic structure calculations.

    Risk-Controlled DFT Selection for Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials#
    作者：Kun Ryu、Yeo Jin Jung、Shyue Ping Ong、Claire Donnat、Ying Shirley Meng。arXiv · stat.ML，2026-10-15
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · 其他AI×科学交叉6方向匹配8
    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：interatomic potential、density functional theory。

    📖 英文原文

    Automated materials discovery requires evaluating large pools of candidate configurations, with density functional theory (DFT) as a major throughput bottleneck. Pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approximate DFT cheaply, but their errors vary by orders of magnitude across configurations, making it difficult to decide when to trust the MLIP predictions and when to fall back to DFT. We frame this decision as a risk-controlled selection problem and solve it within a conformal risk control (CRC) framework. Each configuration is mapped from atomic numbers and Cartesian coordinates to a fixed geometric descriptor, which a lightweight predictor uses to estimate an MLIP-DFT disagreement score in energy, average force, and maximum force. CRC then calibrates this score into a selection threshold for DFT labeling such that the MLIP error on the unselected configurations, which keep their MLIP labels, stays below a user-chosen fraction α of the expected error. Under an identical guarantee across eight dataset-MLIP pairs, our predictor routes 7% and 14% fewer configurations to DFT at α=0.3 than committee baselines. Because committee disagreement overlooks errors the MLIPs share, our gains are largest when the DFT labels and MLIP training data differ in DFT functional and basis set, and narrow when the two agree. Without model access, the structure-only predictor also ranks errors as well as the target MLIP's latent representation and outperforms widely used descriptors such as Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) and Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSF).

    Deep Learning of Phase Transitions for Bilinear-Biquadratic Spin-1 Chains from Correlation Aspects#
    作者：Ming-Chiang Chung、Yu-Ting Kuo、Ian McCulloch、Yuan-Hong Tsai。arXiv · cond-mat，2026-10-15

    📖 英文原文

    We investigate the role of different correlation functions in reconstructing the phase diagram of the spin-1 bilinear-biquadratic chain, parametrized by the angle θ between the bilinear and biquadratic couplings, using our previously developed label-free machine-learning pipeline citeMing21, Ming23, in which an autoencoder compresses iDMRG correlation matrices into latent features and K-means clustering with silhouette analysis groups them into phases. Without any prior labels, the scheme reveals four natural phases, the Haldane, dimerized, critical trimerized, and ferromagnetic phases, and accurately locates the Takhtajan-Babujian (θ=-π/4), SU(3)-symmetric Uimin-Lai-Sutherland (θ=π/4), and Ferro-Dimer (θ=5π/4) transition points; a shallow supervised refinement further sharpens the phase boundaries. We find that different correlation functions play fundamentally different roles in phase identification. The SU(3)-symmetric correlation alone selects a four-cluster partition that can be associated with the four phases, whereas the spin-spin and dimer-dimer correlations must be combined to recover the complete phase diagram. In contrast, the trimer-trimer correlation behaves as a marginal correlation: it is insufficient for phase identification by itself but improves the precision of the predicted phase boundaries when combined with the other correlations. Furthermore, the latent representations and silhouette values reveal signatures that we relate to the θ dependence of the correlations within each phase and, tentatively, to the underlying renormalization-group structure. These results demonstrate that machine learning can not only identify quantum phases but also uncover the physical information encoded in different correlation functions.

    Inferring Transition-Dipoles from Two-Dimensional Infrared Spectroscopy with Deep Learning#
    作者：Hao-Yue Zhang、Rong-Hang Chen、Qing Ai。arXiv · physics，2026-10-15
    🤖 AI×科学 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配5
    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：molecular dynamics。

    📖 英文原文

    Two-dimensional infrared (2D IR) spectroscopy is a powerful nonlinear technique for probing vibrational couplings, anharmonicities, coherence, and molecular dynamics. Among the microscopic parameters encoded in 2D IR spectra, transition dipoles are fundamental quantities that characterize molecular structure and govern light-matter interactions. However, reconstructing these quantities from spectroscopic data remains a challenging inverse problem. Here, we introduce a physics-informed convolutional neural network (CNN), Spec2DipoleNet, trained on 2D IR spectra generated via the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian (NHH) method. This multi-task CNN reconstructs the transition dipole matrices under physically-motivated masking constraints. On the validation set, the model achieves a root-mean-square error (RMSE) of 0.085, an R² of 0.97, and a normalized cross-correlation of 0.98. It reliably recovers matrix elements associated with allowed transitions, while larger deviations are observed for nominally forbidden transitions. Our results suggest that transition dipole operators can be quantitatively inferred from 2D IR spectra, highlighting the potential of physics-informed deep learning for extracting microscopic quantum parameters from multidimensional spectroscopic data.

    Tracking large chemical reaction networks and rare events by neural networks#
    作者：Jiayu Weng、Xinyi Zhu、Jing Liu、Linyuan Lü、Pan Zhang、Ying Tang。arXiv · physics，2026-10-15

    📖 英文原文

    Chemical reaction networks are widely used to model stochastic dynamics in biophysical processes. Their exact probabilistic description via the chemical master equation becomes intractable for high-dimensional systems due to the exponential growth of the state space. This difficulty is further exacerbated when metastable transitions and rare events dominate the dynamics. Here, we present an advanced neural-network approach that effectively tackles large reaction networks and rare-event dynamics within a unified framework. Building on autoregressive representations of the full joint probability distribution, we achieve efficient training through second-order optimization methods, including natural gradient descent and the time-dependent variational principle. We further quantify rare events in spatially extended Schlögl models in both one- and two-dimensional lattices, reaching system sizes with extremely large state spaces up to 3×10¹⁵. These results highlight the potential of neural modeling as a flexible and scalable framework for studying high-dimensional chemical reaction networks and rare events.

    📖 英文原文

    Interpreting electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra commonly requires repeated forward simulation and optimization of spin Hamiltonian parameters. Established software provides mature simulation and fitting methods, but these calculations are not generally available as differentiable components within machine-learning workflows. We present TorchSpin, an open-source PyTorch framework that ports the organization and numerical conventions of EasySpin into a tensor-based computational environment. Representative calculations spanning continuous-wave EPR, ENDOR, pulse EPR, spin dynamics, trajectory simulation, and magnetometry closely reproduced corresponding EasySpin results. Once trained, the CNN produced initial parameter estimates in approximately 1 ms per spectrum while a batch of 512 was processed in approximately 1.3 ms with GPU acceleration. TorchSpin therefore provides both a tested Python implementation of established EPR calculations and a differentiable physical layer for rapid learned inference, spectral refinement, and parameter-label-free training.

Rather than learning the mapping from coarse-grid LES fields to fine-grid DNS, the proposed method learns the mapping from coarse LES fields to the differential-filtered DNS fields.
