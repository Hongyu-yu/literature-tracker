    📖 英文原文

    We investigate how phase information initially stored in a diamond four-level atom is redistributed when the atom interacts with a quantized cavity field and a mechanical mode. The phase φ is encoded in the atomic superposition (|1rangle+eⁱφ|2rangle)/sqrt2, while the optical and mechanical subsystems start in Fock states. The dynamics are generated by a coarse-grained optomechanical Hamiltonian together with normalized non-Hermitian no-jump evolution. A central point of the analysis is that the induced |2rangle↔|3rangle channel contains the distinct photon operators adagger ka^k and a^kadagger k; they cannot be merged without changing the dynamics. The numerical study is deliberately restricted to the stationary, single-photon case k=1, for which the calculation is reproducible and the Hilbert-space truncation can be checked directly. We track the reduced-atomic quantum Fisher information (QFI), von Neumann entropy, purity, and total l₁ coherence. In the parameter range considered, increasing the optomechanical coupling G reduces the maximum conditioned atom--rest entropy from about 1.676 to 0.374 bits and raises the minimum atomic purity from about 0.356 to 0.876. At the same time, the time-averaged local QFI decreases from about 0.450 to 0.336, while the mean total coherence changes only weakly and non-monotonically. Thus stronger optomechanical coupling does not improve all quantum resources simultaneously: it keeps the conditioned atomic state purer and less entangled with the rest, but it also reduces the amount of phase information locally accessible from the atom. To expose this contrast more clearly, the time-dependent observables are plotted for two complementary combinations of G values, and the full nine-point coupling scan is summarized separately.

    📖 英文原文

    Photonic integrated circuits offer compactness, wafer-scale manufacturability and a fast-maturing fabrication base. Here we report what is, to our knowledge, the first fly-inspired directional microphone transduced by integrated photonics: two silicon-nitride microring resonators on opposite wings of a rocking diaphragm, monolithically released above a backside Bosch-etched cavity. Off-axis sound drives the antisymmetric rocking mode, straining one ring in tension and the other in compression; with the laser biased on each ring’s resonance slope, the opposite resonance shifts become two intensity signals of opposite phase, whose difference transduces the direction-bearing motion. The packaged device shows loaded optical quality factors of 18,635 and 17,974 near 1553 nm; scanning laser-Doppler vibrometry resolves the rocking mode near 15.8 kHz and the bending mode near 20.8 kHz. Tone-burst excitation at the two modal resonances demonstrates directional transduction directly in the time domain: at 90° incidence the two ring outputs oscillate in antiphase and their difference is enhanced, whereas at 180° they are in phase and the difference is suppressed. The optical response appears only after the acoustic time-of-flight and rings down after the drive ends, excluding electromagnetic pickup. Finite-element projections of an audio-band device, using shot-noise scaling, indicate an A-weighted signal-to-noise ratio of 74.6 dB(A), more than 10 dB beyond an optimized piezoelectric implementation of the same mechanics. These results constitute a proof-of-concept demonstration of a new transduction platform for chip-scale directional acoustic sensing, with a quantitative roadmap — higher optical quality factor, power— toward practical performance improvement.
