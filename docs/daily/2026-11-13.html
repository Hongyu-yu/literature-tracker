    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：现代机器学习潜力 (MLP) 能够以前所未有的精度实现大规模原子模拟。显式物理相互作用（例如长程静电）的结合不断扩大了它们在化学和材料科学中日益复杂的系统的适用性。然而，将 MLP 扩展到电化学和电催化仍然具有挑战性，因为它需要考虑原子相互作用中的电极电位和电场。此类方法最近才出现，尽管该领域正在迅速发展，但仍需要进一步的概念进步，以便在施加电势下在有限温度下进行可靠的模拟。在这篇综述中，我们全面概述了使用 MLP 进行电气化界面原子模拟的最新进展。当前的发展分为三个不同的类别。第一个包括描述极化、极化率和玻恩有效电荷的线性响应模型，将传统 MLP 扩展到有限电场。第二个包括直接学习场相关势能表面的方法。第三种由一系列将经典模型与机器学习相结合的混合方法组成。尽管仍在积极开发中，但这些方法代表了朝着更真实的模拟接近电子结构计算质量的重要一步。最后，我们讨论了推进基于 MLP 的模拟的开放挑战和可能的未来方向，旨在自下而上地理解电气化接口的原子过程。

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：machine learning potential、electronic structure calculation、born effective charges。

    📖 英文原文

    Modern machine learning potentials (MLPs) enable large-scale atomistic simulations at unprecedented accuracy. The incorporation of explicit physical interactions such as long-range electrostatics has continuously broadened their applicability to increasingly complex systems in chemistry and materials science. Extending MLPs to electrochemistry and electrocatalysis, however, is still challenging as it requires the consideration of electrode potentials and electric fields in the atomic interactions. Such approaches have emerged only recently, and although the field is evolving rapidly, further conceptual advances are still needed for carrying out reliable simulations at finite temperature under applied potential. In this review, we provide a comprehensive overview about recent progress in the atomistic simulation of electrified interfaces using MLPs. Current developments fall into three different categories. The first comprises linear response models that describe polarisation, polarisability, and Born effective charges, extending conventional MLPs to finite electric fields. The second includes methods that directly learn a field-dependent potential energy surface. The third consists of a series of hybrid approaches combining classical models with machine learning. Although still under active development, these methods represent a major step toward more realistic simulations approaching the quality of electronic structure calculations. Finally, we discuss open challenges and possible future directions for advancing MLP-based simulations aiming for a bottom-up understanding of atomistic processes at electrified interfaces.

The results support FNO as a data-efficient baseline for the tested CCP settings, while showing that the preferred architecture depends on the accuracy target and operating-parameter region.

    Predicting experimental vibrational spectra requires both efficient molecular calculations and a reliable mapping from theoretical vibrations to measured signals. Here we introduce HARMONY, a two-stage deep-learning framework that bridges this gap using harmonic spectra as physical anchors. Equivariant neural networks directly predict atomic forces and spectroscopic tensors for rapid harmonic-spectrum construction, while HarmoNet combines these spectra with molecular topology and measurement metadata to predict experimental infrared and Raman spectra. HARMONY achieves mean cosine similarities of 0.912 for infrared and 0.907 for Raman spectra, with a mean inference time of 2.12~s per molecule, approximately 600-fold faster than the evaluated DFT workflow. Beyond forward prediction, HARMONY improves inverse molecular inference through pseudo-experimental pretraining and spectral verification of candidate structures. Integrating HARMONY and Vib2Mol as scientific tools within a general-purpose agent enables adaptive candidate generation and verification. With molecular formulas provided, the resulting Vib2Mol--HARMONY Agent achieves Top-1 recall of 70.53% on NIST using infrared spectra and 83.33% on SDBS using combined infrared and Raman spectra. Fine-tuning with 19 amino-acid controls and eight peptides further increases mean Raman cosine similarity from 0.669 to 0.896 on 15 held-out peptides. HARMONY establishes harmonic anchoring as a practical connection between molecular physics, experimental spectroscopy and adaptive molecular structure elucidation.

    作者：Theo Keane、Anthony Onwuli、Richard Tomsett、Robert M. Forrest、Jonathan J. Bean。arXiv · cond-mat，2026-11-13

    📖 英文原文

    Neural scaling laws enable the prediction of returns from larger training datasets, but machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP) datasets are heterogeneous and expensive to generate, and equal numbers of structures need not contain equal amounts of useful information. We study data scaling under fixed model configurations for M3GNet, MACE, and PET trained on subsets of a combined MatPES-MP-ALOE training pool. Across training structure subsets from 10,000 to 640,000, together with the full 1.16-million-structure training set, we compare uniform random sampling, three chemistry- and structure-informed stratification policies, iterative DIRECT, and furthest-point sampling using embeddings from three pretrained MLIPs. Models are evaluated using cohesive-energy, force, stress, and aggregate losses on both a held-out test set from the parent distribution and the distribution shifted MP-r²SCAN test set. We observe power-law scaling improvements with increasing data, but the fitted scaling exponent, apparent error floor, and relative sample efficiency depend on the model architecture, target quantity, data sampling policy, and test set distribution. Sampling-policy rankings can reverse across test distributions: chemical stratification can improve cross-dataset performance while degrading in-distribution performance, whereas the embedding-space diversity policies frequently under-perform random sampling. Relative loss is strongly associated with test-to-train chemical-space divergence. These results show that structure count is a necessary but insufficient scaling variable for MLIPs. For training MLIPs, the return on additional first-principles calculations depends not only on how many additional structures are included, but also on how their chemical and configurational content aligns with the model's intended use.

    📖 英文原文

    Over the last decade we have built Alexandria, an open database of about 15 million density-functional calculations of crystals. It includes PBE, PBEsol, SCAN and TB-mBJ results, structures relaxed at pressures up to 150 GPa, dielectric tensors and about 145,000 electron-phonon calculations, together with more than 400 million structures relaxed with machine-learned potentials. On these data we trained a generative model for crystal structures, a universal interatomic potential and graph neural networks for more than ten properties, from band gaps to superconducting critical temperatures. From the convex hull we computed a reaction graph of the 17.4 billion reactions between its 193,416 stable compounds. Here we give all of this to a general-purpose language-model agent. Working unattended, the agent screens the database for a solar absorber, drops a candidate whose synthesis releases too little energy, and writes a synthesis plan with precursors, crucible and atmosphere. Asked about the well-studied Ba-Zr-S system, it finds an unreported sulfide and designs the experiment that would confirm or rule it out. Asked for an air-stable alternative, it designs one and tests it against new structures from the generative model. With web search alone, the same agent proposes what the literature already discusses. Put together, the data, the models and the reaction graph let a general agent do what none of them does alone: go from a question in plain words to an experiment that a laboratory can run and test.
