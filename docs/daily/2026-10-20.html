    📖 英文原文

    Diamond is an important host material for functional defects such as solid-state spin qubits. During the defect creation by irradiation and subsequent thermal annealing, carbon-related defects, including vacancies and interstitials, are generated and can migrate to form new defect complexes. To investigate this kinetically driven process and reveal the formation mechanisms of the di-interstitials and di-vacancies in the neutral charge state, we fine-tuned a universal machine learning interatomic potential using a small dataset generated from density functional theory calculations. We obtained a barrier of 1.84 eV for mono-interstitial migration and found that two initially separated mono-interstitials only need to overcome a lower barrier to form di-interstitial configurations. Beyond the training data, the MLIP also identified the formation pathway for an additional di-interstitial configuration and a stepwise divacancy dissociation mechanism. These results reveal migration-assisted defect complex formation mechanisms and demonstrate how targeted MLIP training can identify reaction pathways.

    We present NEP-kappa 2.0, a unified framework that integrates density-functional theory, material-specific machine-learning interatomic potentials, and pretrained force models for advanced phonon calculations.     📖 英文原文

    Universal machine-learning interatomic potentials are increasingly used across diverse materials, yet their reliability under directional deformation remains unclear. We compare compression and extension along paired deformation paths from the same parent structures for eleven pretrained potentials. The benchmark comprises 1,963 Materials Project trajectory (MPtrj) parents and 1,286 experimental-source MinX-Eq parents at six strain magnitudes from 1% to 10%. Density-functional theory provides references for isotropic, uniaxial and biaxial loading in MPtrj and isotropic loading in MinX-Eq. At ± 10%, every model has a larger median force error under compression, with pooled isotropic compression-to-extension ratios of 2.12 and 3.30 for MPtrj and MinX-Eq. Decomposing error growth into DFT force-scale growth and relative-error evolution shows that relative errors decrease on both branches, faster under compression in MPtrj but slower under compression in MinX-Eq. Force-scale matching moves the error ratios toward unity, although MinX-Eq retains a compression-side excess across models. Radial and SOAP analyses place compressed structures at shorter separations and farther from public reference pools, while readout-associated model features show larger compression-side displacements. These results distinguish stronger physical responses from changes in relative predictive accuracy and provide a basis for assessing universal potentials in deformation simulations.
