    The thermophysical response of multilayer heterogeneous thin films under continuous-wave laser heating involves non-uniform volumetric heat generation modulated by interference-induced standing-wave absorption, which governs the layer-by-layer phase-change sequence and the ultimate thermal failure threshold—a coupling that conventional Beer–Lambert approximations fail to capture. This study develops an optical–thermal–ablation coupled model that resolves the standing-wave absorption profile and couples it to temperature-dependent transient heat conduction with latent-heat effects, validated against laser-damage experiments at seven power levels. A two-stage surrogate-based framework is constructed to explore the thermophysical design space in which discrete material choices and continuous layer thicknesses jointly control the absorption distribution and melting sequence. The optimal architecture is identified as SUS304/Ag/Si₃N₄/SiO₂/ITO with a refined thickness profile of 21 µm/0.106 µm/0.18 µm/2.35 µm/0.05 µm under a 25 µm total constraint, yielding a 209.6 K reduction in peak temperature relative to the baseline. The analysis reveals three thermophysical mechanisms: (i) in the interference layer, the order-of-magnitude reduction in volumetric optical absorption dominates over the 2.5-fold difference in thermal conductivity in determining the peak temperature; (ii) the positive temperature coefficient of thermal conductivity in the SUS304 substrate provides a self-regulating thermal-sink effect; and (iii) the Ag layer exhibits a non-monotonic thermal response beyond its optical saturation thickness, governed by the competition between cross-plane thermal resistance and in-plane thermal spreading coupled with absorption-peak shifting. These results clarify how interference-modulated heat generation governs phase-change sequences and thermal failure in multilayer thin films.

    文献研究：Toward chemically accurate quantum simulations on quantum hardware: A quantum-HPC hybrid approach#
    Toward chemically accurate quantum simulations on quantum hardware: A quantum-HPC hybrid approach#
    The Journal of Chemical Physics Current Issue，2026-11-12

    📖 英文原文

    We report one of the most comprehensive experimental demonstrations to date of quantum-computing-based molecular simulation on IQM’s Sirius 24-qubit superconducting quantum processor, using up to 16 operational qubits. We employ Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) with the Local Unitary Cluster Jastrow (LUCJ) Ansatz to compute ground-state energies of benchmark molecular systems, including H 2 , LiH, BeH 2 , H 2 O, and NH 3 . We further introduce the linear-CNOT unitary coupled-cluster singles and doubles Ansatz within the SQD framework, reducing the classical pre-computation overhead at the cost of increased quantum circuit depth and demonstrate its effectiveness by accurately recovering the ground-state energies of H 2 , LiH, and BeH 2 . A comparative analysis of the two Ansätze is presented, highlighting their respective merits, demerits, and practical use cases in near-term quantum simulations. In addition, we perform one-dimensional potential energy surface (1D-PES) scans for H 2 and HeH + using STO-3G and 6-31G basis sets and for LiH and BeH 2 using STO-3G. We also report the first experimental generation of a full two-dimensional potential energy surface (2D-PES) map, consisting of a 32 × 32 grid in bond length and bond angle, for the water molecule in the STO-3G basis on IQM quantum hardware. Beyond small-molecule benchmarks, we integrate Density Matrix Embedding Theory (DMET) with SQD(LUCJ) to obtain chemically accurate 4-electron-in-4-orbital active-space energies for eight ligand-like molecules, as well as for the pharmacologically relevant amantadine molecular system, representing the first of such experimental demonstrations. Across all studies, the majority of quantum-computed energies agree with reference full configuration interaction results and with DMET-complete active space CI energies for embedded systems, to within chemical accuracy for the chosen basis sets. These results demonstrate the robustness of quantum sampling-based classical diagonalization approaches while highlighting the promise of hybrid embedding strategies combined with near-term Ansätze for advancing quantum chemistry toward classically intractable molecular systems and establishing their practicality on current quantum hardware.
