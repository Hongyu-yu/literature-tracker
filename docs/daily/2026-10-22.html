    📖 英文原文

    Predicting molecular magnetism often requires electronic-structure calculations with spin-orbit coupling (SOC), which are costly to repeat across many molecules. The underlying electronic states can transform as spinors, yet equivariant surrogates typically use only integer-rank features. Inspired by the spinor nature of these electronic states, we develop eSpinor, an SU(2)-equivariant graph neural network that propagates spinor features. We initialize spinor features from eigenvectors of an approximate SOC Hamiltonian and use gauge-covariant message passing with a gauge-invariant readout to make predictions independent of the arbitrary eigenbasis choice. On cobalt single-molecule magnet candidates, eSpinor improves zero-field-splitting predictions relative to NequIP, including for unseen ligands. For a dysprosium magnetic resonance imaging probe, eSpinor improves real electron-spin correlations over a Hamiltonian-informed baseline despite similar static crystal-field errors and fewer parameters, while accurately predicting low-field nuclear relaxation in water and after transfer to methanol without retraining. Overall, these results demonstrate that SU(2)-equivariant spinor message passing is a useful inductive bias for molecular magnetism. We also find that similar static tensor errors can yield different dynamical errors, motivating model selection based on accuracy for the intended physical observables.

    📖 英文原文

    We present a model for magnesium-based systems that combines density functional tight binding (DFTB) with MACE, a machine learning interatomic potential (DFTB+MACE). In this model, the conventional repulsive potential, pair potential, is replaced by a many-body MACE potential. The MACE component of the model is trained on the difference between density functional theory (DFT) energies and forces and the corresponding DFTB values, but neglecting the pair potential contribution. Using this model we performed structural relaxation of MgO-CO2 adsorption systems, molecular dynamics calculations of water clusters and phonon spectrum calculations of stable fcc-MgO and metastable bcc-MgO structures. We compare the performance of our model with a pure MACE model and with DFT. We demonstrate that the DFTB+MACE model achieves improved accuracy relative to DFTB with a pair potential, in many cases with only a moderate increase in computational cost. In addition, it can provide electronic structures that most of the machine learning potentials cannot. The training dataset, originally developed for MACE, may not fully represent all regions of the potential surface we may encounter during simulations. Expanding the dataset for a wider potential surface is expected to further enhance predictive accuracy of DFTB+MACE model. Overall, the resulting DFTB+MACE framework enables simulations at length and time scales beyond the reach of first-principles methods while retaining an explicit description of electronic structures, making it particularly attractive for studying charge-transfer in materials.

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：使用量子计算方法预测分子电子结构是计算化学中的一个挑战。传统的变分量子本征求解器 (VQE) 通常会受到优化挑战的阻碍。另一种生成量子本征解算器 (GQE) 通过将基态搜索重新表述为离散序列生成来缓解这一问题，但其 Transformer 主干网络面临着二次计算复杂性的问题，成为较大分子的可扩展性的瓶颈。为了解决这个问题，我们引入了 Mamba Quantum Eigensolver (Mamba-QE)，它用选择性状态空间模型 (SSM) 代替 Transformer，以处理具有线性 O(L) 复杂度的算子序列。此外，我们还集成了距离调节机制，可以直接预测完整的势能面（PES）。在 H2 分子上进行评估后，Mamba-QE 在化学精度范围内重现了完全构型相互作用 (FCI) 参考能量，在 16 个几何形状中实现了低于 3×10⁻⁶ Ha 的能量误差，且变分违规为零。 Mamba-QE 保持高表达能力，同时为复杂分子系统的量子模拟建立强大、可扩展的基础。

    Predicting molecular electronic structure using Quantum Computing approaches is a challenge in Computational Chemistry. Traditional Variational Quantum Eigensolvers (VQEs) are typically hindered by optimization challenges. The alternative Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE) mitigates this by reformulating ground-state search as discrete sequence generation, but its Transformer backbone suffers from quadratic computational complexity, bottlenecking scalability for larger molecules. Evaluated on the H₂ molecule, Mamba-QE reproduces Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) reference energies well within chemical accuracy, achieving an energy error below 3×10⁻⁶ Ha across 16 geometries with zero variational violations. Mamba-QE maintains high expressive power while establishing a robust, scalable foundation for quantum simulations of complex molecular systems.

    📖 英文原文

    Accurate and efficient prediction of lattice thermal conductivity (LTC) is essential for developing materials for thermoelectric, microelectronic, and photovoltaic applications. Here, we present a methodology for high-throughput calculation of LTC across large datasets of compounds sharing a common crystal symmetry. Our approach combines fine-tuning of the MatterSim 1M universal machine-learned interatomic potential with LTC calculations based on the solution of the Boltzmann transport equation. We introduce a symmetry-based few-shot fine-tuning strategy (SBFS-FT) based on low-rank adaptation. In this approach, MatterSim 1M is fine-tuned on a small, carefully selected set of compounds that share a common crystal symmetry. The resulting potential is then applicable for accurate LTC calculations for compounds with this crystal symmetry, even if they were not included in the fine-tuning set. We tested our methodology on datasets of half-Heusler compounds (space group 216), materials with space group 225, and spinels (space group 227). We demonstrated that LTC calculations with MatterSim 1M fine-tuned using SBFS-FT exhibited a mean absolute relative difference from the reference results (obtained using moment tensor potentials and polyMLPs) 2-3 times smaller than that achieved with the non-fine-tuned MatterSim 1M potential. Moreover, the accuracy of LTC calculations with MatterSim 1M after SBFS-FT was comparable to that achieved using the MatterSim 1M potentials individually fine-tuned for each compound. Importantly, SBFS-FT enabled generation of the potential suitable for accurate LTC calculations across large compound datasets substantially faster than fine-tuning of MatterSim 1M for each compound individually, reducing the computational time by approximately a factor of 5-7 for a dataset of 100 compounds.
