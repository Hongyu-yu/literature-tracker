    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：我们开发了点群等变量神经网络，用于学习格系统动态模拟中使用的局部映射。相同的网络作用于每个站点周围的有限邻域，通过共享参数尊重晶格平移。局部配置被分解为根据点组的不可约表示进行变换的特征，隐藏的特征在整个网络中保留该组织。等变量线性映射和对称允许的张量积然后学习非线性空间相关性，同时保持所需的变换定律。这种方法避免了构造一组固定的手工不变描述符，这些描述符的选择需要选择保留哪些局部相关性。它还允许学习映射的复杂性在不扩大其空间截止值的情况下增长。我们通过学习方格荷斯坦模型中控制绝热动力学的力来演示该构造。与精确对角化的比较检验了预测力的准确性和空间相关性的演变。力评估随系统尺寸线性缩放，允许训练模型模拟大晶格上的电荷密度波排序。这些模拟再现了电荷密度波域的异常缓慢粗化，说明了学习的局部映射用于集体动力学。

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：equivariant neural network。

    📖 英文原文

    We develop point-group equivariant neural networks for learning local mappings used in dynamical simulations of lattice systems. The same network acts on a finite neighborhood around each site, respecting lattice translations through shared parameters. The local configuration is decomposed into features that transform according to irreducible representations of the point group, and hidden features retain this organization throughout the network. Equivariant linear maps and symmetry-allowed tensor products then learn nonlinear spatial correlations while preserving the required transformation laws. This approach avoids constructing a fixed set of hand-crafted invariant descriptors, whose selection requires choices about which local correlations to retain. It also allows the complexity of the learned mapping to grow without enlarging its spatial cutoff. We demonstrate the construction by learning the forces governing adiabatic dynamics in the square-lattice Holstein model. Comparisons with exact diagonalization test the accuracy of the predicted forces and the evolution of spatial correlations. Force evaluation scales linearly with system size, allowing the trained model to simulate charge-density-wave ordering on large lattices. These simulations reproduce anomalously slow coarsening of the charge-density-wave domains, illustrating the use of the learned local mapping for collective dynamics.

    Quantum systems governed by time-dependent Hamiltonians pose significant challenges for the accurate computation of their unitary time-evolution operators. In this work, we introduce a deep learning approach based on physics-informed neural networks to estimate these operators over the full time domain, either by predicting the time-evolution operator directly or by predicting an effective Hamiltonian from which that operator is computed. Unitarity is imposed as a constraint in the first case, and holds by construction in the second, where a propagation layer based on the second-order Magnus expansion or Trotterization maps the predicted Hamiltonian to the evolution operator. The model is trained on characterization data from a small number of times, generated numerically here but standing in for measurements on a system whose Hamiltonian is unknown to the model. The loss combines three contributions: agreement with pairs of input and evolved states, agreement with the evolution operator at the sampled times when it is available, and a unitarity constraint that requires no measured data. Systems of 2 to 8 qubits are considered, with a Hamiltonian containing all non-identity Pauli components up to 6 qubits, used as a baseline test against random dense matrices, and physically motivated nearest-neighbor Ising and Heisenberg models beyond. Across all system sizes, and using a limited number of sampled times, a statistical analysis over independent Hamiltonian realizations gives mean gate fidelities close to with low variability. Comparable fidelities are obtained when the model is trained from state data alone, without providing the evolution operator at any sampled time during training. Once trained, the model interpolates the evolution operator with inference times to lower than those of a numerical interpolation baseline based on geodesic interpolation, depending on system size and propagation method. These results support the use of the approach for data-driven simulation and identification of quantum dynamical systems.
