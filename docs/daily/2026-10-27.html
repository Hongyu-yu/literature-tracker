    用于机器学习原子间势的热力学校正层#

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：机器学习原子间势能够在大长度和时间尺度上以接近从头开始的精度进行原子模拟。基础模型将这种能力扩展到整个元素周期表，但继承了用于生成训练数据的电子结构方法的热力学描述。因此，相对相位稳定性的小误差可能会导致转变温度的大误差。我们引入了附加热力学校正层，该层可将相自由能移向评估参考，而无需重新训练或修改基础电势。一阶热力学结构将所需的相自由能位移直接转换为构型能量目标，该目标使用线性原子团簇展开进行拟合，并根据测量的自由能迭代细化。我们使用基础 GRACE 模型将该方法应用于具有不同晶体结构的元素之间的熔化和固体到固体的转变。校正后，所有转变温度均位于评估值的规定公差范围内，包括 Ca、Ti 和 Zr，其中固相转变和熔化一起校正。静态能量曲线、体积模量和液体结构仍然接近基础模型。空位形成能、热膨胀和液体密度取决于校正后的相平衡以及校正较大时的变化。该方法既不需要重新训练、可微分动力学，也不需要访问基础势的参数。它提供了一种将可转移原子模型与评估相热力学相结合的模块化途径。

    📖 英文原文

    Machine learning interatomic potentials enable atomistic simulations with near ab initio accuracy over large length and time scales. Foundation models extend this capability across the periodic table but inherit the thermodynamic description of the electronic structure methods used to generate their training data. Small errors in relative phase stability can therefore produce large errors in transformation temperatures. We introduce an additive thermodynamic correction layer that shifts phase free energies towards an assessed reference without retraining or modifying the base potential. A first order thermodynamic construction converts the required phase free energy shifts directly into configuration energy targets, which are fitted using a linear atomic cluster expansion and refined iteratively from measured free energies. We apply the method to melting and solid to solid transformations across elements with different crystal structures using a foundation GRACE model. After the correction, all transformation temperatures lie within the prescribed tolerance of the assessed values, including Ca, Ti, and Zr, where a solid to solid transition and melting are corrected together. Static energy curves, bulk moduli, and liquid structure remain close to those of the base model. Vacancy formation energies, thermal expansion, and liquid density depend on the corrected phase balance and change where the correction is large. The method requires neither retraining, differentiable dynamics, nor access to the parameters of the base potential. It provides a modular route for combining transferable atomistic models with assessed phase thermodynamics.

    Crystal generative models mainly learn what stable crystals look like, with little explicit supervision for what makes them stable.     作者：Alessandro Sinibaldi、Luciano Loris Viteritti、Riccardo Rende、Federico Becca、Giuseppe Carleo。arXiv · cond-mat.supr-con，2026-10-27

    📖 英文原文

    Materials design is an inverse problem: finding the couplings of a microscopic Hamiltonian whose ground state exhibits a desired property. For strongly correlated quantum systems, this remains largely out of reach, as each candidate Hamiltonian requires its own costly simulation. Here we show that Foundation Neural-Network Quantum States, single wave functions that encode the ground states of an entire family of Hamiltonians, make this search efficient. Because the Hamiltonian couplings enter as neural-network inputs, any ground-state property can be optimized by gradient descent directly in coupling space. We use this approach to search for nonmagnetic phases in two-dimensional spin models in coupling spaces of increasing dimensionality. Specifically, we focus on quantum spin liquids, exotic phases of matter in which quantum fluctuations prevent magnetic order at zero temperature. In the largest search space considered, spanned by eight superexchange couplings and containing many paradigmatic frustrated spin systems, the optimization converges to the J₁-J₂-δ Heisenberg model when starting from a magnetic phase. We show that the ground state at the optimum set of couplings has neither magnetic nor valence-bond order in the thermodynamic limit, leading to a new quantum spin-liquid candidate. The approach is completely general and can be extended to other models and physical properties to optimize, demonstrating that Neural-Network Quantum States are tools not only for simulating quantum matter but also for designing it.

    In solid acid electrolytes CsH₂PO₄ and CsHSO₄, the origin of fast proton conduction has long been debated, attributed either to local proton hopping or to polyanion rotation. However, the precise role of polyanion rotation and its interplay with proton hopping remained unresolved. Nanosecond-scale molecular dynamics simulations, driven by equivariant neural network force fields, reveal a nuanced proton slingshot mechanism: protons are first carried by rotating polyanions, followed by O-H bond reorientation, and the combined motion enables long-range jumps. This mechanism challenges the conventional revolving paddlewheel model, revealing long-range transport arises from significant O-H bond reorientation assisted by only limited polyanion rotation. Despite their structural similarity, CsH₂PO₄ and CsHSO₄ exhibit qualitatively different transport behavior, governed by their distinct proton concentrations. In CsH₂PO₄, two rotation rates with different activation energies emerge, while CsHSO₄ displays a single-rate process with faster productive polyanion dynamics. The higher proton concentration in CsH₂PO₄ frustrates PO₄ rotation, resulting in slower dynamics than for SO₄ in CsHSO₄. In CsH₂PO₄, we further identify a unique correlation between oxygen sharing and proton transport, arising from the extra proton per polyanion. Together, our findings establish a unified framework linking polyanion rotation, proton coordination, and long-range transport, and suggest that tuning proton concentration could accelerate rotation and enhance conductivity.
