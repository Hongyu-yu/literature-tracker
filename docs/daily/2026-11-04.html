    📖 英文原文

    Excited-state intramolecular proton transfer is a prototypical ultrafast process that forms the foundation for many chemical and biological phenomena and is highly sensitive to changes in the underlying potential energy landscape. Here, we develop neural network potentials for 10-hydroxybenzo[h]quinoline that describe ground- and excited-state dynamics with near ab initio accuracy. These neural network potentials enable efficient sampling of the proton transfer coordinate and incorporation of nuclear quantum effects through path-integral molecular dynamics. The free energy profiles show that excited-state intramolecular proton transfer proceeds on a largely barrierless excited-state surface, with nuclear quantum effects reducing and smoothing the residual barrier. Non-equilibrium simulations yield an average proton transfer time of ∼11.5 fs for quantum hydrogen, consistent with experiment, and reveal a clear isotope effect, with slower dynamics for deuterium and classical nuclei. Mechanistic analysis identifies transient proton donor–acceptor contraction as the key enabling coordinate, while the proton crosses the midpoint prior to reaching the minimum of this coordinate, indicating a dynamically driven transfer. Subtle changes in the conjugated backbone precede proton transfer, reflecting early electronic reorganization and coherent coupling between proton motion and backbone vibrations. Bond-length alternation and vibrational coherence analysis reveal isotope-dependent coherence loss, with deuteration producing stronger damping and more localized proton/backbone dynamics relative to hydrogen. These results establish neural network potential-based approaches as an effective framework for simulating excited-state proton transfer with explicit nuclear quantum effects.

    📖 英文原文

    Long-range interactions in machine-learning interatomic potentials are commonly treated by coupling learned sources through either a prescribed electrostatic kernel or a learned but configuration-independent one. Whether such a kernel suffices depends on the underlying screened interaction. We address this question by using Fourier neural operators (FNOs) to learn how latent source fields, built from the descriptors of a local backbone (here MACE), interact across the simulation cell. For a synthetic ionic system with tunable screening, a prescribed Coulomb kernel underestimates the long-wavelength charge fluctuations by 43% under strong screening, whereas the FNO reproduces them within 3% and recovers 95-99% of the variance of the residual left by the local backbone. A linear FNO, which learns a configuration-independent kernel, performs equally well in the homogeneous case. Under inhomogeneous screening, rearranging the remote medium modulates the coupling between distant ions, a dependence that no configuration-independent kernel can represent. Among the models tested, only the nonlinear FNO responds to this modulation, and its force errors are 27-41% lower than those of configuration-independent models. In first-principles benchmarks of liquid water, lithium lanthanum zirconate, and Au2/MgO(001), MACE-FNO is comparable to a prescribed-kernel model for the bulk systems and more accurate for the interface. It further captures the reversal of the Au2 wetting preference induced by remote Al dopants, with deviations below 5 meV per cell from density functional theory. These results indicate that a configuration-dependent kernel, as learned by FNOs, becomes necessary once the screening medium varies, even though the energy remains quadratic in the charges for a fixed medium.

    Foundation machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) offer one model for many chemistries, but their deep, globally coupled architectures do not fit the spatial decomposition that scales classical and short-ranged MLIPs. We present pair_style uma, a LAMMPS pair style that evaluates the Universal Model for Atoms (UMA) through LibTorch on NVIDIA and Intel GPUs from a single source tree. It reimplements FairChem's edge-sharded graph parallelism in C++ over exported TorchScript modules, with one MPI rank per device, NCCL or oneCCL collectives, and the whole energy and force path in double precision. The strain-gradient virial is carried through the exported modules and the cross-device reductions, so that fix npt runs on both vendors' hardware. We measure NPT capacity and cost on one 4xA100-40GB node, one 8xH200 node, and one 12-tile Intel node, and on up to 32 Intel nodes.
