    Porous silicon (pSi) is a highly promising material for nanoscale thermal management, but predicting its thermal conductivity remains challenging due to complex structure-property dependencies. In this work, we present a comprehensive multiscale investigation of heat transport in pSi, combining experimental gas-microphone photoacoustic (PA) measurements with atomistic Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations and continuum-level Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). Mesoporous pSi layers with porosities ranging from 48% to 75% were fabricated via electrochemical etching of highly doped p+-type silicon, and their effective thermal conductivity was experimentally determined. Our findings reveal that the thermal properties are fundamentally governed by the initial substrate doping, which dictates the formation of either isotropic sponge-like or anisotropic columnar morphologies. We demonstrate that MD simulations accurately capture the thermal behavior of heavily doped samples by explicitly accounting for phonon confinement, whereas the PINN approach excellently models percolation and macroscopic heat flux in stochastic networks typical of lightly and moderately doped silicon. By integrating these computational and experimental datasets with literature values, we utilized symbolic regression to derive a generalized analytical expression for the effective thermal conductivity of pSi as a function of porosity and temperature. This hybrid multiscale framework connects nanoscale phonon mechanics to macroscopic thermophysical properties, providing a predictive tool for the design of porous silicon-based thermoelectric and thermal-insulation devices.

    Bismuth vanadate (BiVO4) is a benchmark oxide photoanode whose performance is governed by surface states, yet the thermodynamics and charge transition levels (CTLs) of its surface defects remain far less characterized than its bulk defect physics. Here we use dielectric-dependent hybrid density functional theory to compute formation energies and CTLs for oxygen vacancies and for substitutional n-type (Mo, W) and p-type (Ca, Sr) dopants on BiVO4(010). Under the O-rich conditions of anodic operation, Mo and W substitute on the V sublattice, which they prefer over the Bi site by 3.3 and 3.7 eV for a Fermi level at the conduction band edge. Surface oxygen vacancies are shallow donors whose ε(2+/0) level lies 0.16 eV below the conduction band and are ionized over almost the whole gap. MoV and WV are deeper donors at 0.51 and 0.58 eV below the conduction band. CaBi is a shallow acceptor 0.02 eV above the valence band, whereas SrBi has no transition within the gap and is ionized at every Fermi level. The two acceptors also place the hole differently, CaBi on the outermost oxygen layer and SrBi in the interior. Because photoanode operation is limited by holes reaching the surface, an acceptor that captures photogenerated holes on the outermost oxygen layer should be more useful for water oxidation than one that leaves them in the interior, which makes CaBi the more promising p-type dopant of the two.

    In this work, experimental data were obtained confirming unusual contribution of dipole-dipole interaction between unpaired electron of the radical and its own ¹⁵N nucleus (DD1) to EPR resonance line shapes in solutions of ¹⁵N-Tempol radicals. One might expect that the random modulation of DD1 by rotational diffusion leads to spectral diffusion of the resonance frequencies: DD1 randomly flips the orientation of the nitrogen nuclear spin, causing the electron spin to randomly change frequencies and jump from one hyperfine component to another. Spectral diffusion is expected. This is an expected process in the case of the interaction of a nuclear spin with the lattice or with electrons in other radicals, or in the case of spin diffusion within the nuclear spin system. In this case, the electron spin facilitates flipping of the nuclear spin, and resonant frequency of that electron changes. As Marsh demonstrated, the elementary process is not limited merely to the nuclear spin flip. In addition to the frequency shift, spin coherence of unpaired electron acquires an extra phase of π. In this work, we present experimental data demonstrating negative interference between contributions of DD1 and DD2 paramagnetic relaxation mechanisms, on the one hand, and paramagnetic relaxation caused by spin exchange during random bimolecular collisions of radicals, on the other. Spin exchange leads to spectral diffusion without π-phase jumps of spin coherence.
