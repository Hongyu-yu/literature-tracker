    作者：Sanaz Alikhah、Daegeun Jo、Peter M. Oppeneer。arXiv · cond-mat，2026-11-24

    📖 英文原文

    Magneto-optical signals in nonmagnetic semiconductors entangle spin and orbital contributions, both in angular-momentum generation and magneto-optical detection. We calculate these contributions for electron-doped GaAs and Si using density-functional theory, linear-response theory, and a second-order density-matrix treatment of the inverse Faraday effect. At small electron doping, the intrinsic orbital Hall conductivity exceeds its spin counterpart in magnitude, while the orbital contribution to light-induced magnetization dominates over broad photon-energy ranges. We show that orbital- and spin-resolved optical Hall and polar Kerr coefficients exhibit stronger sensitivity to orbital moments than to equal spin moments. This contrast reflects direct electric-dipole coupling to orbital motion versus spin-orbit-mediated coupling to spin polarization. Consequently, Si maintains a substantial orbital magneto-optical response despite its weak spin-orbit interaction. At probe energies of 1.5 eV in GaAs and 3.0 eV in Si, the magnitude of the Kerr rotation per unit orbital moment is approximately 5 and 13 times larger than that per unit spin moment, respectively. Combining the calculated Hall conductivities and Kerr coefficients with a phenomenological drift-diffusion model yields predominantly orbital edge signals for the assumed diffusion constants and relaxation lengths. These results indicate that optical detection of induced magnetization in doped semiconductors such as GaAs and Si can be dominated by orbital signals, and therefore cannot be interpreted solely in terms of spin polarization.
