    📖 英文原文

    Biochar has emerged as a promising material for remediating toxic hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)), yet the distinct roles of its soluble components and solid matrix remain poorly understood. This study systematically investigated the mechanisms of Cr(VI) immobilization by rice husk biochar produced at different pyrolysis temperatures (200–600°C). The biochar pyrolyzed at 400°C (RH400) exhibits the highest Cr(VI) reduction efficiency, effectively converting Cr(VI) to less toxic Cr(III). Through comparative experiments with biochar leachate and the residual solid matrix, we demonstrate that the soluble leachate does not contribute to Cr(VI) reduction, but instead antagonizes total chromium immobilization by competing for sorption sites. In contrast, the washed solid residue (WRH400) serves as the primary active component, achieving a 2.4-fold higher total Cr immobilization capacity than pristine RH400. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) analysis identifies carbon-centered persistent free radicals (PFRs) as the key electron donors for Cr(VI) reduction. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) further reveals that this reduction process is accompanied by oxidation of the biochar matrix, with a shift of PFRs from carbon-centered to carbon/oxygen-centered species. This work conclusively identifies the biochar solid matrix and its associated PFRs as the dominant drivers for Cr(VI) reductive immobilization and suggests that pre-washing biochar can optimize its performance in environmental remediation.

    📖 英文原文

    The conformational heterogeneity of amyloid-β42 (Aβ42) may influence its early interactions with lipid membranes, yet the extent to which different initial structures determine subsequent membrane association remains unclear. Here, three nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) derived Aβ42 monomer structures (1IYT, 1Z0Q, and 6SZF) were examined at a 1-palmitoyl-2-oleoyl-sn-phosphocholine (POPC) membrane-water interface. Despite an identical environment, the monomers followed different trajectories during comparative 200-ns all-atom molecular dynamics simulations using the CHARMM36m force field.
