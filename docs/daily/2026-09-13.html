    📖 英文原文

    Rain-induced cracking is among the most damaging preharvest disorders of sweet cherry because even narrow cuticular fractures reduce fresh-market value and create entry sites for water loss and fungal infection. Conventional inspection is subjective and is least reliable for low-contrast microcracks near the pedicel cavity. This study developed a Fourier-guided multi-scale Vision Transformer (FGM-ViT) for joint fruit-level severity grading and pixel-level crack segmentation. The dataset comprised 960 individual fruit from four commercial cultivars and eight orchard blocks, imaged from four rotational views after natural rainfall exposure or controlled water-immersion challenge. Crack severity was assigned as intact, microcrack, moderate, or severe using stereomicroscopic measurements of cumulative crack length, maximum width, affected surface area, and crack location. All views of an individual fruit and all observations from the same orchard block were constrained to the same fold in a nested five-fold grouped evaluation. FGM-ViT combined a multi-scale spatial encoder with luminance-normalised Fourier residual tokens and frequency-guided cross-scale attention. The complete model achieved 93.8 ± 0.9% fruit-level accuracy, 93.7 ± 1.0% macro-F1, and 0.842 ± 0.018 Dice coefficient for crack segmentation. It exceeded the spatial-only transformer by 2.2 percentage points in accuracy and 2.2 points in macro-F1, with the largest gain observed for microcracks. Classification errors were restricted almost entirely to adjacent severity categories. Under pedicel overlap, glare, brightness shifts, and motion blur, FGM-ViT retained a 2.7–3.5-point advantage over the spatial-only model. Frequency-band occlusion indicated that mid-to-high spatial frequencies carried complementary evidence for narrow fractures, whereas spatial features remained essential for crack location and marketability interpretation. The dual-output framework provides an auditable, low-cost RGB approach for sweet-cherry sorting, cultivar screening, and rain-cracking phenotyping.

    📖 英文原文

    Cross-regional box office forecasting is routinely evaluated with predictive accuracy on randomly split samples, yet the models that minimize root mean squared error are not necessarily the models that produce better market selection decisions. This paper develops a decision-outcome validation framework in which forecast models are compared through a budget-constrained market selection problem, using temporal walk-forward holdouts, region-out and film-out generalization protocols, and top-k payoff, regret and hit rate as the evaluation criteria. The framework is applied to a web-sourced panel of 14 Chinese films released between 2023 and 2025, covering 76 film market pairs and 18 overseas territories with weekly gross data. Five models are compared, including a panel model with market fixed effects, regularized regression, gradient boosting and a decision-aware ranking model. The accuracy-optimal and decision-optimal models diverge in 22 of 30 validation folds. In the temporal fold covering the 2025 releases, the decision-optimal model captures 89.3 percent of the achievable revenue share, against 88.7 percent for the accuracy-optimal model and 78.9 percent for the boosting model that the weekly accuracy rankings favored, and the payoff gap between decision-based and accuracy-based selection is 152,746. Accuracy differences between models are statistically significant in three of the four temporal folds, and the decision ranking is robust to regularization strength, tree depth and the outcome window, while stronger regularization improves accuracy but lowers decision payoff. Paired bootstrap intervals for the payoff difference between decision-based and accuracy-based selection are positive across random and film-out folds, although the small number of folds limits statistical power. A Monte Carlo study with heteroscedastic market errors shows that divergence between accuracy and decision rankings is systematic when overall accuracy is balanced, and disappears only when one model dominates on both criteria. The results imply that accuracy-oriented validation with random splits is not a neutral protocol for cross-regional forecasting, and that decision outcomes should be reported alongside conventional error metrics.
