    Flexible sensors serve as the core component of wearable electronics and intelligent health monitoring devices, possessing enormous application potential in the fields of human physiological signal acquisition and motion behavior detection. Nevertheless, flexible sensing materials generally suffer from poor environmental stability, weak anti-interference capability of sensing signals, and inevitable degradation of sensing performance during long-term cyclic operation, which severely restrict their large-scale practical applications. To address these issues, waterborne polyurethane (WPU) is adopted as the flexible matrix, modified MXene nanosheets with KH560 and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) were introduced as hybrid conductive fillers. An integrated high-performance flexible strain sensor is fabricated via a solution blending and casting strategy. Through hydrogen bonding and electrostatic adsorption effects, a 1D-2D interconnected conductive network is constructed, where MXene acts as the primary conductive unit and MWCNTs function as interconnection bridging segments. This flexible sensor based on the composite system exhibits superior comprehensive performances, including a high gauge factor of 2.81, a broad strain detection range of 0-2800%, rapid response and recovery times of 150 ms and 120 ms, as well as excellent cycling stability over 3000 cycles. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations elucidate the interfacial mechanism of the MXene/MWCNTs heterojunction, verifying that the sensing mechanism originates from the synergistic effect of band structure evolution and conductive network reconstruction. Furthermore, the sensor enables real-time monitoring of electromyographic signals during human movements, demonstrating great application potential in intelligent wearable electronics.
