    📖 英文原文

    Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) offer near– ab initio accuracy at a fraction of the cost of conventional electronic structure calculations, but their reliability depends critically on the quality and diversity of training data. Here, we present a classical-to-density functional theory (DFT) multi-fidelity workflow for efficient dataset generation, in which long classical molecular dynamics trajectories are supplemented with selectively DFT-optimized configurations to train neural network potentials. We demonstrate this approach on four systems: bulk water, water at the air–water interface, CaSO 4 in bulk water, and CaSO 4 at the interface. In all cases, the trained models faithfully reproduce reference DFT properties, validating the workflow across both simple and chemically complex environments. We further probe the extrapolation capabilities of the models, showing that bulk-trained networks can generalize to different temperatures, pressures, and interfacial geometries when datasets include targeted augmented configurations. This multi-fidelity strategy thus enables accurate and transferable MLIPs for complex solvated and interfacial systems, bridging the gap between computational efficiency and first-principles accuracy.

    We present a machine-learning (ML) charge density model, DeltaChargeNet, integrated with the full-potential tight-binding muffin-tin orbital (FP-TB-MTO) method for electronic structure and quantum transport simulations of materials and devices. The ML model predicts electron density from atomic structures with low linear-scaling cost, while FP-TB-MTO provides an accurate tight-binding representation of the system Hamiltonian. To achieve high-fidelity ML density for complex devices with interfaces and disorder, we train an equivariant graph convolutional network to predict Δ ρ-the difference between DFT and atomic superposition densities-under a charge neutrality constraint required for inhomogeneous systems. Finally, we demonstrate quantum transport in a 11220-atom infrared detector. By combining first-principles accuracy with linear-scaling inference, DeltaChargeNet enables large-scale device simulations without the full DFT self-consistent field loop.

    Classical phase-field models of brittle fracture capture toughness-controlled crack growth but not the material's strength surface, which governs crack nucleation. The theory of Kumar et al. (2020) incorporates the strength surface through a nucleation driving force, and Chockalingam (2025) derived this driving force for arbitrary strength surfaces described by n material parameters, with which the phase-field strength surface (PFSS) converges to the material strength surface as the regularization length ε → 0 and matches it exactly at n chosen states for any ε. By decoupling strength fidelity from the regularization length, the approach substantially reduces the mesh resolution required for phase-field fracture simulations of large structures.
