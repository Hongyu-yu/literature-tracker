    用于开发水系统机器学习潜力的经典到 DFT 多保真方法#
    Classical-to-DFT multi-fidelity approach for developing machine learning potentials of aqueous systems#
    The Journal of Chemical Physics Current Issue，2026-11-16
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配8

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：机器学习原子间势（MLIP）以传统电子结构计算成本的一小部分提供近乎从头算的精度，但其可靠性主要取决于训练数据的质量和多样性。在这里，我们提出了一种用于高效数据集生成的经典到密度泛函理论 (DFT) 多保真工作流程，其中长经典分子动力学轨迹辅以选择性 DFT 优化配置来训练神经网络潜力。我们在四个系统上演示了这种方法：散装水、空气-水界面处的水、散装水中的 CaSO 4 和界面处的 CaSO 4 。在所有情况下，经过训练的模型都会忠实地再现参考 DFT 属性，从而在简单和化学复杂的环境中验证工作流程。我们进一步探讨了模型的外推能力，表明当数据集包含目标增强配置时，批量训练的网络可以推广到不同的温度、压力和界面几何形状。因此，这种多保真度策略能够为复杂的溶剂化和界面系统提供准确且可转移的 MLIP，从而缩小计算效率和第一原理准确性之间的差距。

    🔗 与我们工作的关联与启发

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：machine learning potential、electronic structure calculation、density functional theory、neural network potential。

    📖 英文原文

    Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) offer near– ab initio accuracy at a fraction of the cost of conventional electronic structure calculations, but their reliability depends critically on the quality and diversity of training data. Here, we present a classical-to-density functional theory (DFT) multi-fidelity workflow for efficient dataset generation, in which long classical molecular dynamics trajectories are supplemented with selectively DFT-optimized configurations to train neural network potentials. We demonstrate this approach on four systems: bulk water, water at the air–water interface, CaSO 4 in bulk water, and CaSO 4 at the interface. In all cases, the trained models faithfully reproduce reference DFT properties, validating the workflow across both simple and chemically complex environments. We further probe the extrapolation capabilities of the models, showing that bulk-trained networks can generalize to different temperatures, pressures, and interfacial geometries when datasets include targeted augmented configurations. This multi-fidelity strategy thus enables accurate and transferable MLIPs for complex solvated and interfacial systems, bridging the gap between computational efficiency and first-principles accuracy.

    作者：Qingyun Zhang、Yang Hu、Aixia Zhang、Zehua Sun、Jiawei Yan、Xiaohao Zhou 等 8 人。arXiv · cond-mat.supr-con，2026-11-16

    关联：与你方向的关键词重合：electronic structure。

    📖 英文原文

    We present a machine-learning (ML) charge density model, DeltaChargeNet, integrated with the full-potential tight-binding muffin-tin orbital (FP-TB-MTO) method for electronic structure and quantum transport simulations of materials and devices. The ML model predicts electron density from atomic structures with low linear-scaling cost, while FP-TB-MTO provides an accurate tight-binding representation of the system Hamiltonian. To achieve high-fidelity ML density for complex devices with interfaces and disorder, we train an equivariant graph convolutional network to predict Δ ρ-the difference between DFT and atomic superposition densities-under a charge neutrality constraint required for inhomogeneous systems. A secondary ML residual charge model compensates for errors in the primary Δ ρ model. The predicted density is then used within the full-relativistic FP-TB-MTO framework combined with the non-equilibrium Green's function method. We validate our method on bulk HgCdTe disordered alloys (including vacancy defects) and Au/HgCdTe/Au disordered devices. The maximum band energy error is below 10 meV for bulk HgCdTe. For devices, the charge neutrality constraint suppresses long-wavelength charge bias that would otherwise distort the electrostatic potential by 0.68-1.88 eV near the device center, while residual compensation corrects the remaining 0.16-0.46 eV potential mismatch, essential for reproducing the correct potential barrier profile. Together, these corrections reproduce the layer-resolved potential, including interface steps and bulk-like plateaus, within 0.1 eV of fully self-consistent DFT. Finally, we demonstrate quantum transport in a 11220-atom infrared detector. By combining first-principles accuracy with linear-scaling inference, DeltaChargeNet enables large-scale device simulations without the full DFT self-consistent field loop.

    Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) methods provide a way to compute molecular ground-state energies, but their accuracy and runtime depend sensitively on configuration choices such as the subspace dimension, propagation timestep, and the allocation of a fixed sampling budget across circuits, for which no formal selection method exists. In this work, we explore whether oracle models can predict the quantum runtime and approximation error from the Hamiltonian and the candidate configuration. Using synthetic Hamiltonians with controllable spectral properties, we show that machine-learned models can map a Hamiltonian directly to the best-ranked configuration within a predefined grid for the SKQD algorithm, validated against classical Krylov propagation, achieving strong rank correlations between true and predicted error and runtime, Spearman ρ = 0.969 and ρ = 0.905, respectively. Extending the approach to 50 molecular Hamiltonians (20-50 qubits), with reference ground-state energies from classical calculations, we train regressors that predict the simulation error and runtime for a given molecule--configuration pair ahead of execution, and classifiers to distinguish, from 600 SqDRIFT quantum-hardware runs, whether a configuration achieves a ground-state energy within chemical accuracy (1.6×10⁻³ Ha approx 1 kcal/mol). For the molecular Hamiltonians, the models additionally provide an a priori feasibility and resource estimate, predicting whether a molecule can reach chemical accuracy on current hardware and at what runtime, thus correctly classifying held-out molecules at the molecule level, achieving balanced accuracy 61.8% in-distribution and 100% for five larger held-out molecules.

    作者：Ryan T. Grimm、Michael Lindsey。arXiv · physics，2026-11-16

To facilitate rapid averaging, we use a moment-generating function to evaluate the order parameter, lowering the cost to a single tensor contraction. We obtain high-fidelity estimates for the critical parameters by averaging over 500,000 disorder realizations.
