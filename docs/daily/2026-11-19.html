    Universal machine learning interatomic potentials (UMLIPs) offer accuracy close to first-principles calculations at a fraction of the cost, showing significant potential for large-scale material simulations. However, the fragmented UMLIPs ecosystem lacks unified interface standards and integration frameworks, hindering their automated deployment in high-throughput workflows. To address this, we present UniMatSim, a modular Python framework. The framework enables seamless model switching via abstracted interfaces, incorporates task orchestration, and provides standardized modules for key properties (elasticity, phonons, molecular dynamics), including automated handling for low-dimensional materials. Results show UniMatSim significantly improves computational efficiency and reproducibility, providing a reliable infrastructure for data-driven materials discovery and design.

    Photopolymerization of acrylated epoxidized soybean oil (AESO) is a convenient model of a self-organizing process in a soft, bio-based material: ultraviolet light starts a free-radical chain reaction, the growing network releases heat, and the mobility of molecular groups changes on several time scales at once.     📖 英文原文

    Grain boundaries in perovskites exhibit a rich variety of electronic, polar and magnetic properties, yet identifying their atomic structures and compositions remains challenging. Here, we combine the Grand Canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) method with a universal machine-learning interatomic potential to explore the coupled compositional and structural phase space of Σ5(310)[001] SrTiO₃ grain boundaries. The resulting chemical potential phase diagram identifies ten stable structures belonging to four motif families: large Sr kite, small Sr kite, incomplete Sr large kite, and Ti-rich structures. Extending the GCMC method to the Sigma5(210)[001] and Sigma17(410)[001] grain boundaries reveals how grain boundary plane and inclination angle influence the competition between small- and large-kite motifs. Density functional theory calculations of the predicted Sigma5(310)[001] grain boundaries show that the composition and atomic structure strongly govern the spatial distribution of conduction and valence band edge states, interfacial band alignment, and carrier localization. Four distinct electronic regimes emerge: localized electron-doped, delocalized electron-doped, insulating, and hole-attracting states. Our electrostatics analysis reveals the coexistence of the short-range electric fields at the reconstructed GB core associated with local atomic density and bonding, and the longer-range fields outside the core arising from the space charge. These fields can also couple to local polar distortions and generate composition-dependent polarization patterns. These results establish an efficient framework for exploring oxide perovskite grain boundary structures and reveal how stoichiometry and atomic reconstruction couple to electronic structure, electrostatics, and local polarization, providing a route toward predictive grain boundary engineering in functional perovskites.
