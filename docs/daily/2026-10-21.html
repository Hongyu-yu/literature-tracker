    具有可学习轨道对矩阵因子的神经网络分数量子霍尔态：平衡物理先验和灵活性#

    作者：Yubing Qian、Weiluo Ren、Ji Chen。arXiv · cond-mat，2026-10-21
    🔬 神经网络势·电子结构 · AI×物理AI×科学交叉8方向匹配6

    📄 摘要（中文翻译）：神经网络分数量子霍尔 (FQH) 态为捕获朗道级 (LL) 混合和理解 FQH 物理提供了一种有前途的替代方案，但迄今为止，物理先验的结合仅限于 ansatz 中内置的最小精确约束。在球面几何中，我们表明，当仅提供最小的附加物理先验时，以及当严格地施加诸如通量附加之类的先验时，网络的灵活性都会受到影响。在前一种情况下，单极子谐波积轨道（MHPO）模拟很难优化，而在后一种情况下，神经网络的表达能力受到限制。因此，我们提出了轨道对矩阵因子 MHPO (MHPO-M) ansatz，它不仅用可学习的对因子替换固定的 Laughlin 结构，而且使它们依赖于电子构型和轨道，从而平衡物理先验与灵活性。在 ν=1/5、1/3 和 2/5 范围内，MHPO-M 在测试的变体中实现了最低的变分能量，并且基于它的因式分解轨道读数可以扩展到 35 个电子，其能量与从 3-LL 精确对角化结果推断出的趋势具有竞争力。这些结果表明，可以在不牺牲神经网络波函数灵活性的情况下适当地合并物理先验，从而为具有 LL 混合的 FQH 系统的精确、可扩展建模开辟了一条有希望的道路。

    📖 英文原文

    Neural network fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states offer a promising alternative for capturing Landau level (LL) mixing and for understanding FQH physics, yet the incorporation of physical priors has so far been limited to the minimal exact constraints built into the ansatz. Working in spherical geometry, we show that the network's flexibility suffers both when only minimal additional physical priors are supplied and when priors such as flux attachment are imposed rigidly. In the former case, the monopole harmonic product orbital (MHPO) ansatz is difficult to optimize, whereas in the latter, the expressivity of the neural network is restricted. We therefore propose the orbital pair matrix factor MHPO (MHPO-M) ansatz, which balances physical priors against flexibility by not only replacing the fixed Laughlin structure with learnable pair factors but also making them dependent on both the electron configurations and the orbitals. Across ν=1/5, 1/3, and 2/5, MHPO-M achieves the lowest variational energy among the tested variants, and a factorized orbital readout based on it enables scaling to 35 electrons, with energies competitive with trends inferred from three-LL exact-diagonalization results. These results demonstrate that physical priors can be appropriately incorporated without sacrificing the flexibility of neural network wavefunctions, opening a promising path toward accurate, scalable modeling of FQH systems with LL mixing.

    Ammonia and hydrogen are major constituents of planets within and beyond the solar system, yet how they interact in planetary envelopes is poorly known. We perform large-scale two-phase coexistence simulations of the ammonia-hydrogen system from 500 to 4000 K and 0.1 to 100 GPa, driven by machine-learning interatomic potentials trained on density functional theory calculations. From these we map the compositions of coexisting hydrogen-rich and ammonia-rich phases, and the critical curve that separates conditions of complete miscibility from those of phase separation. The critical temperature increases with pressure, from 500 K at 0.1 GPa to 2000 K near 30 GPa, and we find no phase separation at 3000 and 4000 K up to 100 GPa. The slope of the critical curve decreases substantially between 0.3 and 30 GPa, and the curve nearly flattens above about 30 GPa and 2000 K, where molecules begin to give way to atomic species. The coexistence curves are asymmetric with an ammonia-rich critical composition. For the expected envelope compositions, the phase boundary lies close to, and intersects, published thermal profiles of Uranus and Neptune, implying ammonia may be raining out in these planets today. In contrast, in warm sub-Neptunes such as TOI-270 d, ammonia and hydrogen are likely miscible in all proportions. Together with previous studies, our results suggest that silicates, water and ammonia separate from hydrogen in sequence as planets cool, with implications for their compositional, thermal and structural evolution.

    Understanding how water wets graphene is critical for predicting and controlling its behaviour in nanofluidic, sensing, and energy applications. A key measure of wetting is the contact angle made by a liquid droplet against the surface, yet experimental measurements for graphene span a wide range, with no consensus for free-standing graphene. Here, we use a machine learning potential with ab initio accuracy to provide an atomistic first-principles prediction for this unsolved problem, finding a weakly hydrophilic contact angle of 72.1 ± 1.5 deg. We show that there is a strong coupling between the three-phase contact line and the intrinsic thermal ripples of free-standing graphene, which contributes to this strain sensitivity. Our results demonstrate that the wettability of 2D membranes are governed not only by their chemistry but also by their dynamic morphology, introducing a new class of wetting behaviour unique to atomically thin materials that offers an additional explanation for variability in experimental measurements. These findings reveal that mechanical strain may be a practical route to controlling wetting in 2D nanomaterials-based technologies, with promising consequences for nanofluidic and nano-filtration applications.
