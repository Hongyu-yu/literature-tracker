    📖 英文原文

    Chemical bonding is the language through which chemistry rationalizes materials structure, stability, properties, and reactivity. Meanwhile, modern deep-learning models achieve remarkable predictive performance using representations based largely on compositional and structural information, raising the question of whether explicit bonding concepts remain necessary. In this work, we argue that artificial intelligence does not render chemical bonding obsolete but provides a new framework for evaluating and refining bonding concepts. We show that, through linear probing, Allegro and MACE embeddings recover bonding descriptors with (R² > 0.95), indicating that bonding emerges naturally from Allegro's and MACE's latent spaces. We also show that bonding descriptors provide information beyond bond distance in our electronic-structure-featurized MEGNet (ESF-MEGNet) model, reducing prediction error for properties including bulk and shear moduli by 15%. We discuss implications for interpretable machine learning, synthesizability prediction, and generative materials design, and suggest joint-embedding approaches for learning more general bonding representations. More broadly, we propose that chemical bonding can be understood as a learnable representation of electronic structure. From this perspective, different bonding models represent alternative compressions of the underlying electronic information and can be evaluated through their predictive power, dimensionality, and complexity. The age of machine learning shifts the focus from whether a bonding model is physically motivated to whether it captures the information required to explain and predict materials behavior. If a materials genome exists, it should be encodable through a representation of chemical bonding. If no bonding representation can capture this information, our understanding of the chemical bond may require revision.

    We introduce UnIPH, an equivariant graph neural network designed to jointly predict both interatomic potentials and tight-binding (TB) Hamiltonians for an atomic structure. UnIPH is trained using density functional theory (DFT) calculations followed by Wannier projection to predict energies and forces as node-level targets and Wannier-projected TB Hamiltonians as edge-level targets. This enables physically consistent modeling of coupled mechanical-electronic responses with near-DFT-level accuracy. After separately training two UnIPH models on DFT data for bilayer graphene and monolayer MoS2, respectively, we find that these models capture key deformation-electronic effects: in twisted bilayer graphene, the trained UnIPH reveals how interlayer spacing, in-plane strain, and out-of-plane corrugation drive isolated flat-band formation, and further shows that modulating substrate interaction strength can generate flat bands even away from the magic angle (approximately 1.1 degrees). For monolayer MoS2, UnIPH accurately reproduces phonon dispersions and strain-dependent band-gap evolution, and further predicts how a non-uniform substrate creates localized strain pockets that spatially modulate the conduction- and valence-band edges.
